II. LEGAL STANDARD

A Single Justice may grant interlocutory relief under Mass. R. App. P. 6 upon consideration of four factors: (1) the likelihood of success on the merits; (2) whether denial of the stay will cause irreparable harm; (3) whether the stay will substantially harm other parties; and (4) where the public interest lies. Packaging Indus. Group, Inc. v. Cheney, 380 Mass. 609, 617 (1980). The moving party need not demonstrate a probability of success; a serious question is sufficient where the other factors strongly favor relief. GTE Prods. Corp. v. Stewart, 414 Mass. 721, 723 (1993).

Courts have consistently recognized that homelessness, medical deterioration, and deprivation of liberty constitute irreparable harm. Packaging Indus. Group, 380 Mass. at 617.

C. No Substantial Harm to Other Parties

The Personal Representative holds estate assets. Order #100 directed payment of $2,000 and up to $10,000 — amounts that, in the context of an estate valued at multiple hundreds of thousands of dollars, are de minimis. Compliance with an existing court order causes no legally cognizable harm to the Personal Representative. She has had twenty days to comply and has not done so.

D. The Public Interest Favors Relief

The public interest is served by the enforcement of court orders, the protection of immunocompromised and homeless litigants from irreparable medical harm, and the assurance that pro se litigants are not entrapped in procedural loops they cannot exit. The public interest is not served by a probate court directing a litigant to file a contempt motion and then denying it, by denying evidence of documented medical harm, or by scheduling a non-judicial settlement conference at which a physically compromised party will be pressured to waive rights.
