III. BACKGROUND

This complaint arises from the same estate proceeding described in the concurrent BBO complaint against Robert C. Lawless, Esq. (Docket No. BA22P1104EA, Barnstable Probate and Family Court). Attorney Theodore A. Schilling appears on the certificate of service of multiple filings in this matter and has received all court filings from Complainant by email service. He is listed as counsel to the Personal Representative or in a related representative capacity.

IV. SPECIFIC RULE VIOLATIONS

A. Rule 3.4(c) — Knowing Facilitation of Non-Compliance with Court Order

Rule 3.4(c): "A lawyer shall not… knowingly disobey an obligation under the rules of a tribunal except for an open refusal based on an assertion that no valid obligation exists."

Attorney Schilling received copies of Order #100 (April 10, 2026) and all subsequent correspondence by email service. He is of record in the case. His office received and was copied on correspondence documenting that Order #100 has not been complied with for twenty days. There is no indication that Attorney Schilling filed a motion to stay the order, sought a modification, or advised his client to comply. His passive receipt of notice of ongoing non-compliance — without taking any step to bring his client into compliance with a court order — constitutes participation in that non-compliance.

B. Rule 8.4(d) — Conduct Prejudicial to the Administration of Justice

Rule 8.4(d): "It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to… engage in conduct that is prejudicial to the administration of justice."

As co-counsel or estate counsel, Attorney Schilling has a duty to ensure that court orders in proceedings in which he appears are complied with. The failure to take any corrective action — advising compliance, filing a motion, or withdrawing from representation of a non-compliant client — is prejudicial to the administration of justice when the non-compliance causes ongoing irreparable harm to a homeless, immunocompromised opposing party.

C. Rule 1.2(d) — Counseling or Assisting Criminal or Fraudulent Conduct

Rule 1.2(d): "A lawyer shall not counsel a client to engage, or assist a client, in conduct that the lawyer knows is criminal or fraudulent."

To the extent that Attorney Schilling was aware of, or participated in the preparation of, filings with the Probate Court that falsely represented the status of Complainant's bank account information or that accepted or allowance estate accounts reflecting fraudulent transactions, he may have counseled or assisted conduct that is fraudulent within the meaning of Rule 1.2(d). Complainant requests that the BBO investigate the full scope of Attorney Schilling's participation in the estate administration.

V. VERIFICATION AND REQUEST FOR INVESTIGATION

Complainant acknowledges that some of the specific facts regarding Attorney Schilling's conduct are within the knowledge of the attorney's client and office. Complainant therefore respectfully requests that the BBO conduct a full investigation, including review of the Barnstable Probate Court docket (BA22P1104EA) and the correspondence between counsel, and determine the full scope of Attorney Schilling's participation in the conduct described herein.

II. BACKGROUND

Stuart Rapp, Esq. served as estate attorney for the Estate of Kenneth L. Ventura, Sr. and participated in the drafting and structuring of the trust instrument and estate accounts that are at issue in Docket No. BA22P1104EA. Complainant alleges that Attorney Rapp's conduct in connection with the estate administration — including the allowance of accounts, the involvement of shell companies, and the use of the decedent's identity in transactions not authorized under the will — constitutes violations of the Massachusetts Rules of Professional Conduct and may constitute criminal conduct subject to parallel proceedings.

III. SPECIFIC RULE VIOLATIONS

A. Rule 1.7 / Rule 1.8 — Conflict of Interest

Rule 1.7: "A lawyer shall not represent a client if the representation involves a concurrent conflict of interest."
Rule 1.8: "A lawyer shall not… use information relating to the representation of a client to the disadvantage of the client."

To the extent that Attorney Rapp structured the trust instrument or allowance of accounts in a manner that benefited entities in which he had an interest — including shell companies or related business entities receiving estate distributions — he had a conflict of interest that was not disclosed to Complainant as a beneficiary and was not waived by all interested parties. An estate attorney who structures transfers that benefit himself or entities associated with him violates Rule 1.7.

B. Rule 1.15 — Safekeeping of Client Property

Rule 1.15: "A lawyer shall hold property of clients or third persons that is in a lawyer's possession in connection with a representation separate from the lawyer's own property."

If Attorney Rapp participated in the preparation or allowance of estate accounts that reflected transfers to shell entities — whether or not he personally received those funds — his preparation and certification of those accounts constitutes a failure to safeguard estate property in the interest of all beneficiaries, including Complainant.

C. Rule 8.4(b) and (c) — Criminal Conduct and Fraud

Rule 8.4(b): "It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to… commit a criminal act that reflects adversely on the lawyer's honesty, trustworthiness or fitness as a lawyer in other respects."
Rule 8.4(c): "It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to… engage in conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, deceit or misrepresentation."

Complainant alleges that estate accounts submitted to the Probate Court for allowance under Attorney Rapp's involvement contain material misrepresentations regarding the disposition of estate assets. To the extent that any account reflected transactions using the decedent's identity or authorization after death, or transactions to entities not named in the will, those accounts are fraudulent. An attorney who prepares, certifies, or submits fraudulent accounts to a probate court violates Rule 8.4(b) and (c) and may be subject to criminal liability under M.G.L. c. 266 § 37 (forgery/fraud) and related statutes.

D. Rule 3.3(a) — False Statements in Accounts Submitted to the Court

Rule 3.3(a)(1): "A lawyer shall not knowingly… make a false statement of fact or law to a tribunal."

If Attorney Rapp submitted or caused to be submitted estate accounts containing false representations of asset values, distributions, or the identity of recipients, those submissions constitute false statements to a tribunal within the meaning of Rule 3.3(a)(1).

IV. REQUEST FOR INVESTIGATION

Complainant requests that the Board of Bar Overseers conduct a full investigation into Attorney Stuart Rapp's conduct in connection with the Estate of Kenneth L. Ventura, Sr. (Docket No. BA22P1104EA), including:

  1. Review of all estate accounts submitted to the Barnstable Probate Court for allowance;
  2. Review of the trust instrument drafted by or under Attorney Rapp's supervision for conflicts of interest;
  3. Identification of any shell companies or entities receiving estate distributions that are affiliated with Attorney Rapp;
  4. Coordination with the Barnstable County District Attorney's office and the Massachusetts Attorney General's office regarding any parallel criminal investigation;
  5. Such disciplinary action as is warranted, up to and including disbarment.
