V. LEGAL FRAMEWORK — WHY THIS CMC CANNOT BIND PETITIONER

This is a non-binding mediation proceeding. Trial Court Rule VIII, which governs Pathways CMCs, does not authorize a JCM to enter enforceable orders. Only a judge can enter an order that binds the parties. Nothing that happens at this CMC is enforceable unless it is reduced to a written agreement signed by Petitioner voluntarily and then endorsed by a judge as a court order.

Duress voids consent. Under Massachusetts law, a contract or agreement entered under duress is voidable. International Underwater Contractors, Inc. v. New England Tel. & Tel. Co., 8 Mass. App. Ct. 340, 342 (1979). The combination of homelessness, imminent arrest, medical crisis, and financial deprivation caused by the Personal Representative's non-compliance with Order #100 constitutes duress as a matter of law. Any agreement signed under these conditions is not voluntary.

"Agree or wait 5–6 months" is coercive framing, not a legal ultimatum. The Personal Representative's counsel's representation that Petitioner must either accept the trust or wait for trial is not a legal ruling — it is a litigation position. Petitioner has the absolute right to take his case to trial. He has already waited four years. Choosing to wait for a fair trial is not obstruction.
