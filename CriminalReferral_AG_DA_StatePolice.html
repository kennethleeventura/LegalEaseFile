III. CRIMINAL ACTS ALLEGED

A. Larceny by Embezzlement — M.G.L. c. 266, § 30

M.G.L. c. 266, § 30: Whoever steals or with intent to defraud embezzles, fraudulently converts, or takes and carries away property of another… shall be punished by imprisonment in the state prison for not more than five years.

The estate of Kenneth L. Ventura Sr. has been administered for more than four years. Complainant is entitled to a distributive share of that estate. During those four years, the Personal Representative has: (1) failed to distribute Complainant's inheritance; (2) submitted accounts to the Probate Court for allowance that Complainant contends reflect fraudulent transfers; and (3) directed or permitted estate assets to flow to entities not authorized under the will. The withholding of inheritance funds by a personal representative who has legal control over those funds, for years beyond any legitimate administrative purpose, and in defiance of court orders directing distribution, constitutes larceny by embezzlement.

B. Identity Fraud — M.G.L. c. 266, § 37E; 18 U.S.C. § 1028

M.G.L. c. 266, § 37E: Whoever, with intent to defraud, poses as another person without the express authorization of that person and uses such person's personal identifying information to obtain or attempt to obtain money, credit, goods, services, anything of value, any identification document, or to harass another shall be guilty of identity fraud.
18 U.S.C. § 1028: Federal identity theft statute — applies when identity fraud occurs in connection with mail or wire communications crossing state lines.

Complainant alleges that transactions in or associated with the estate have been conducted using the identity or authorization of Kenneth L. Ventura Sr. after his death, without lawful authorization from the estate, in connection with entities or accounts not identified in the will. The use of a deceased person's identity for financial transactions — including the authorization of estate distributions to shell companies — constitutes identity fraud under state and federal law. Because Complainant is located in California and all estate communications crossed state lines, federal identity theft jurisdiction is established.

C. Forgery / Fraud on the Court — M.G.L. c. 266, § 37

M.G.L. c. 266, § 37: Whoever forges or counterfeits… any instrument purporting to be the act of another… with intent to injure or defraud, shall be punished.

If estate accounts submitted to the Probate Court for allowance misrepresent the identity of recipients, the value of assets, or the nature of transactions — and those accounts were prepared or certified by attorneys with knowledge of those misrepresentations — those submissions constitute fraud on the court and may constitute forgery of court instruments.

D. Wire Fraud and Mail Fraud — 18 U.S.C. §§ 1341, 1343

18 U.S.C. § 1343 (Wire Fraud): Whoever, having devised or intending to devise any scheme or artifice to defraud, transmits or causes to be transmitted by means of wire, radio, or television communication in interstate or foreign commerce… shall be fined or imprisoned.

All email communications between counsel in this matter cross state lines (Massachusetts to California). The pattern of false representations — including the false statement that Complainant provided no bank account for wire transfer, made via email — constitutes wire fraud. The submission of fraudulent estate accounts by mail or electronic filing constitutes mail fraud. Each false communication is a separate predicate act.

E. RICO — 18 U.S.C. § 1962 / M.G.L. c. 271, § 39

18 U.S.C. § 1962(c): It shall be unlawful for any person employed by or associated with any enterprise engaged in, or the activities of which affect, interstate or foreign commerce, to conduct or participate, directly or indirectly, in the conduct of such enterprise's affairs through a pattern of racketeering activity.

RICO requires: (1) an enterprise; (2) a pattern of racketeering activity (two or more predicate acts within ten years); (3) the defendant's participation in the enterprise's affairs through that pattern; (4) effect on interstate commerce. All elements are met here:

  1. Enterprise: Angela L. Ventura, Robert C. Lawless, Theodore A. Schilling, Stuart Rapp, and associated shell companies or entities constitute an enterprise operating under the guise of the estate administration;
  2. Pattern of racketeering: Multiple acts of wire fraud (false email representations), mail fraud (fraudulent court filings), identity fraud (use of decedent's identity), and larceny (embezzlement of estate assets) — each a predicate act — spanning more than four years;
  3. Participation: Each named party participated in the enterprise's affairs: Lawless and Schilling through legal representation facilitating non-compliance; Rapp through trust structuring and account allowance; Angela Ventura as the Personal Representative executing distributions;
  4. Interstate commerce: Established by wire communications between Massachusetts and California and by the interstate transfer of estate funds.
