I. PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

Plaintiff Kenneth L. Ventura Jr. brings this action for violations of his constitutional rights under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and § 1985, for violations of the federal RICO statute (18 U.S.C. § 1962), and for violations of the Americans with Disabilities Act (42 U.S.C. § 12132). This action does not ask this Court to reverse any judgment of the Barnstable Probate and Family Court. It asks this Court to redress independent constitutional violations committed by state actors and private parties acting in concert with them — violations that no state court ruling can authorize and that exist apart from any probate determination.

Plaintiff is a disabled individual (HIV-positive, CD4 count 14%) who has been homeless since November 2024 as a direct consequence of Defendants' ongoing fraud and the state court system's deliberate failure to enforce its own orders against Defendants. The deprivations Plaintiff has suffered — of property, liberty, due process, equal protection, and access to justice — are ongoing, irreparable, and federally cognizable.

II. JURISDICTION AND VENUE

This Court has subject matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 (federal question), 28 U.S.C. § 1343 (civil rights), 18 U.S.C. § 1964(c) (civil RICO), and 42 U.S.C. § 12133 (ADA). Venue is proper in this District under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b) because a substantial part of the events giving rise to these claims occurred in Barnstable County, Massachusetts.

Rooker-Feldman Safe Harbor

These claims are framed as independent federal violations, not as appeals of state court judgments. The basis of each claim is the Defendants' conduct — not the state court's rulings. See Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Saudi Basic Indus. Corp., 544 U.S. 280 (2005) (Rooker-Feldman applies only to claims that are inextricably intertwined with state court judgments; independent injuries are cognizable). The state court's denial of motions without findings, its facilitation of a due-process trap, and the private Defendants' RICO conduct are injuries independent of any probate judgment.

V. CLAIMS FOR RELIEF

Count I — 42 U.S.C. § 1983: Deprivation of Due Process (14th Amendment)

42 U.S.C. § 1983: Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or usage of any State… subjects, or causes to be subjected, any citizen of the United States to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party injured.

Defendants, acting under color of state law, deprived Plaintiff of his constitutional right to procedural due process in the following independent respects:

  • The state court directed Plaintiff to file a contempt complaint (through its JCM), then denied that filing without findings — eliminating every enforcement mechanism available to Plaintiff;
  • The state court denied three motions on April 30, 2026, without any stated findings, depriving Plaintiff of the ability to understand, respond to, or appeal the court's reasoning;
  • Private Defendants acting in concert with the state court (Lawless, Schilling, Rapp) used the court process as an instrument of deprivation — a form of state action under Dennis v. Sparks, 449 U.S. 24 (1980);
  • A court order (Order #100) directing immediate property rights was nullified by the Defendants' non-compliance and the court's refusal to enforce it — depriving Plaintiff of his property interest in the estate distribution without due process.

Count II — 42 U.S.C. § 1983: Equal Protection (14th Amendment)

The Barnstable Probate Court systematically accepted bare, unsubstantiated objections from the represented Personal Representative ("we object") while denying Plaintiff's detailed, documented motions without explanation. The court accepted late-filed objections (December 2025 to July 2025 affidavits) from the represented party while denying Plaintiff's timely filings. This pattern of asymmetric treatment of similarly situated parties based on their represented/unrepresented status constitutes a denial of equal protection.

Count III — 42 U.S.C. § 1985: Conspiracy to Deprive Civil Rights

42 U.S.C. § 1985(3): If two or more persons… conspire… for the purpose of depriving… any person or class of persons of the equal protection of the laws… the party so injured or deprived may have an action for the recovery of damages.

Defendants Angela L. Ventura, Robert C. Lawless, Theodore A. Schilling, and Stuart Rapp conspired to deprive Plaintiff of his property rights and equal protection of the laws through coordinated non-compliance with court orders, submission of fraudulent estate accounts, false representations to the tribunal, and the use of estate assets to fund entities associated with the conspiracy. The conspiracy operated under the guise of legitimate estate administration to conceal the deprivation.

Count IV — 18 U.S.C. § 1964(c): Civil RICO

Defendants constitute an enterprise within the meaning of 18 U.S.C. § 1961(4). Their conduct of that enterprise's affairs through a pattern of racketeering activity (wire fraud, mail fraud, identity fraud, and larceny — each a predicate act under § 1961(1)) over more than four years satisfies the requirements of 18 U.S.C. § 1962(c). Plaintiff has been injured in his business and property by reason of this violation within the meaning of § 1964(c), entitling him to treble damages, costs, and attorney's fees.

Count V — 42 U.S.C. § 12132: Americans with Disabilities Act

42 U.S.C. § 12132: No qualified individual with a disability shall, by reason of such disability, be excluded from participation in or be denied the benefits of the services, programs, or activities of a public entity, or be subjected to discrimination by any such entity.

Plaintiff is HIV-positive with a CD4 count of 14% — a qualifying disability under the ADA. The Barnstable Probate Court, as a public entity, failed to provide Plaintiff with meaningful access to the court system commensurate with his disability status. The court's failure to enforce Order #100 — which directed housing assistance and immediate funds for an immunocompromised homeless litigant — and the court's refusal to admit medical evidence (the photo booklet) documenting disability-related harm, constitute exclusion from the benefits of court services by reason of disability.
