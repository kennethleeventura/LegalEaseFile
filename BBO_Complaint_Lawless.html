III. BACKGROUND

This complaint arises from the estate administration of Kenneth L. Ventura, Sr. in Barnstable Probate and Family Court (Docket No. BA22P1104EA), where Attorney Robert C. Lawless represents Personal Representative Angela L. Ventura. Complainant Kenneth L. Ventura Jr. is the decedent's son, a primary beneficiary, and appears pro se.

The estate has been open for more than four years. Complainant is currently homeless and severely immunocompromised (HIV-positive, CD4 count 14%) as a direct result of the Personal Representative's refusal to distribute estate funds. On April 10, 2026, the Probate Court entered Order #100 requiring immediate partial distribution ($2,000 by direct deposit) and housing assistance (up to $10,000 to a landlord). As of April 30, 2026 — twenty days after entry of that order — Attorney Lawless has facilitated the Personal Representative's complete non-compliance.

IV. SPECIFIC RULE VIOLATIONS

A. Rule 3.4(c) — Knowing Disobedience of a Court Order

Rule 3.4(c): "A lawyer shall not… knowingly disobey an obligation under the rules of a tribunal except for an open refusal based on an assertion that no valid obligation exists."

Order #100, entered April 10, 2026, is a court order with the force of law. Attorney Lawless's correspondence of April 14, 2026 — four days after the order — demonstrates knowing non-compliance. His email states: "Naturally, this will cause further delay." This is not an assertion that no valid obligation exists. It is an acceptance — in writing — that delay in compliance will occur. Attorney Lawless did not file a motion to stay Order #100, did not seek a modification, and did not move for clarification. He accepted delay as an outcome. Twenty days passed without compliance.

B. Rule 3.3(a)(1) — False Statement to a Tribunal

Rule 3.3(a)(1): "A lawyer shall not knowingly… make a false statement of fact or law to a tribunal."

In communications to the court and to Complainant, Attorney Lawless stated that Complainant "did not provide a bank account that could accept a wire transfer." This statement is false and demonstrably so. Complainant provided three bank account options to Attorney Lawless's office via email to Deborah Nardone at 9:23 AM on April 10, 2026 — the same day Order #100 was entered. Attorney Lawless had this information when he made the contrary representation four days later. This is a knowing false statement of fact.

C. Rule 8.4(c) — Conduct Involving Dishonesty, Fraud, Deceit, or Misrepresentation

Rule 8.4(c): "It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to… engage in conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, deceit or misrepresentation."

The false bank account representation described above — made in writing, four days after the true facts were established in email correspondence — constitutes misrepresentation within the meaning of Rule 8.4(c). The misrepresentation served to justify non-compliance with Order #100 and to place the appearance of fault on Complainant for the Personal Representative's non-performance.

D. Rule 1.3 — Failure to Act with Reasonable Diligence (Client Complicity)

Rule 1.3: "A lawyer shall act with reasonable diligence and promptness in representing a client."

While Rule 1.3 primarily applies to a lawyer's duties to their own client, an attorney who knowingly counsels a client to disobey a court order — rather than advising compliance, seeking a stay, or moving for modification — acts in a manner inconsistent with the attorney's obligations as an officer of the court. Attorney Lawless's correspondence indicates that he was aware of Order #100, was aware of the compliance deadline, and did not advise his client to comply or seek judicial relief from the obligation. Instead, he accepted delay as a matter of course.

E. Rule 8.4(d) — Conduct Prejudicial to the Administration of Justice

Rule 8.4(d): "It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to… engage in conduct that is prejudicial to the administration of justice."

A court order requiring immediate payment to a homeless, immunocompromised beneficiary was left unperformed for twenty days while opposing counsel accepted "further delay" in writing. Meanwhile, Complainant — who cannot survive without this distribution — was denied every motion he filed to enforce the order. The administration of justice is directly prejudiced when a represented party's attorney facilitates non-compliance with court orders to the physical detriment of the opposing unrepresented party.
